# The Neighbours

HedgeDoc sits in a crowded field —
shared documents, wikis, notebooks.
These are characterisations from the
projects' own documentation, not
benchmarks; the lab ran HedgeDoc only.

## Etherpad: the ancestor in the room

Etherpad is the original realtime pad:
many typists, one document, nothing
else. It writes in something close to
plain text on screen — formatting is
light, export is the escape — and it is
smaller and simpler to run than
HedgeDoc. If your group truly only
needs the shared page, Etherpad is the
honest minimum. HedgeDoc is what
happened when the pad grew Markdown, a
rendered reading view, accounts,
permissions and slides: more moving
parts (chapter 2), more document at
the end of the meeting.

## HackMD: the sibling who went to work

HedgeDoc *is* HackMD's community
edition, continued in the open after
the two projects separated. HackMD the
service is the polished commercial
relative: hosted workspaces, teams,
integrations, a bill. A group that
outgrows a self-run instance — or never
wants to run one — will find the family
resemblance total, because it is not a
resemblance. This guide exists for the
other choice: the same pad, on your
machine, under your hand.

## The cloud documents

Google Docs and Microsoft 365 sell the
far end of the trade: word processors
in the browser, comments and
suggestions, office formats, enormous
polish — inside their accounts, on
their computers, under their terms.
For a document that must be a *Word
document* at the end, they are simply
the tool. HedgeDoc's bet is the
opposite on every axis: Markdown
instead of office formats, a server of
yours instead of a tenancy, a link
instead of an account list. Its pads
are plainer and more portable; the
cloud's documents are richer and less
yours.

## BookStack: the wiki

BookStack (which has its own guide in
this series) is where documents go to
be *kept*: shelves, books, chapters and
pages, organised on purpose, written
one editor at a time. It has no live
multi-typist pad and wants none —
curation is its whole idea. The two
compose naturally: draft together in a
HedgeDoc pad at the speed of the
meeting, then shelve the settled text
in BookStack at the speed of thought.
Chapter 9's advice — keep the addresses
of pads you care about somewhere you
control — is most often a BookStack
page.

## Joplin and Obsidian: the notebooks

Joplin and Obsidian (each with a guide
in this series) are notebooks for one
mind: your notes, on your machines,
organised for retrieval by their
author. HedgeDoc's pads are the
opposite posture — one text, many
authors, kept by a server. People
routinely use both: the notebook for
what is *mine*, the pad for what is
*ours, right now*. Nothing in either
tool imports the other's live state,
and the bridge is the honest one:
Markdown downloads, which chapter 9
showed are always plain text.
